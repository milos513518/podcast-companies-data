In this episode, Sagnik Nandy (CTO & EVP of Engineering @ Docusign) joins Jerry to discuss keys to success when it comes to peer-led AI transformation. Sagnik shares how they approached AI adoption internally @ Docusign with the use of both champions & multipliers, understanding trade offs associated with AI adoption, determining which tasks to assign to agents, frameworks for measuring the ROI of agentic tools, and how it helps bring value to your customers. He also discusses how to measure the customer productivity index (CPI) and what it takes to mitigate customer issues when scaling AI internally.
ABOUT SAGNIK NANDY
Sagnik Nandy is the Chief Technology Officer and Executive Vice President of Engineering at Docusign responsible for engineering, cloud operations, and technology strategy that enables customers globally to run their organizations on Docusign.
Most recently, Sagnik served as the President and Chief Development Officer for Workforce Identity at Okta where led engineering development and operations, product management, and design and user research. Earlier, he joined Okta as their CTO, overseeing the engineering and business technology arms of the company.
Prior to Okta, Sagnik served as the Vice President of Engineering at Google where he oversaw several critical components of Google’s core advertising business, the backbone of the company’s growth and revenue. During his 15 years at Google, Sagnik also helped build Google’s Measurement and Analytics offerings and took several products from inception to becoming market leaders.
Sagnik has a bachelor’s degree from the BITS, Pilani (Birla Institute of Technology and Science Pilani) and a Ph.D. in computer science and engineering from UC San Diego.
The CAFE(S) framework is a research-backed definition of context quality for AI agents.
DX collaborated with researchers at Capital One, Google, and UVic to give teams a shared standard for reviewing agent context by breaking it into five properties: clarity, actionability, fidelity, efficiency, and security.
Your agent is only as good as its context and the CAFE(S) framework establishes a shared language around context to help you better understand how & why agents go wrong.
