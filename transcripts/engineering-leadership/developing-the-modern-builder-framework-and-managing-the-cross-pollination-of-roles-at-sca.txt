DESCRIPTION (publisher's show notes):
In this episode, Pratima Arora, Chief Product and Technology Officer @ Smartsheet, dives into what it means to be a modern builder as product, design, and engineering roles converge into today’s post-AI world. She and Jerry discuss why the modern builder model is disrupting traditional engineering processes / responsibilities as individuals embrace a breadth of skills while maintaining individual expertise. They cover how to encourage “cross-pollination” of roles, maintaining product quality while improving iteration speed, facilitating bottom-up contributions, creating context / knowledge graphs to inform agents, examples of good product builders, and what this means for hiring.
ABOUT PRATIMA ARORA
Pratima Arora is the Chief Product and Technology Officer at Smartsheet. In this position, she is responsible for the company’s product management, product marketing, user experience, pricing and packaging, and digital and product partnerships.
An accomplished technology executive, Pratima has more than 20 years of experience as a product and technology leader with a passion for building innovative tools that solve real problems.
She has held senior product leadership roles at Atlassian and Salesforce, working on some of the companies’ highest-revenue products. Most recently, she was Chief Product and Technology Officer at Chainalysis, where she led the global R&D organization for one of the world’s top blockchain data platforms.
She holds a B.S. in Physics, an M.S. in Computer Science, and an MBA from UC Berkeley’s Haas School of Business.
The CAFE(S) framework is a research-backed definition of context quality for AI agents.
DX collaborated with researchers at Capital One, Google, and UVic to give teams a shared standard for reviewing agent context by breaking it into five properties: clarity, actionability, fidelity, efficiency, and security.
Your agent is only as good as its context and the CAFE(S) framework establishes a shared language around context to help you better understand how & why agents go wrong.
